I am a researcher at **VSB – Technical University of Ostrava**, working with the eHealth Lab on radar-based physiological monitoring. My work sits where clinical medicine, digital health, and artificial intelligence meet — and most of it comes down to one question: what can a signal tell us about a body that the body itself cannot say out loud?

I hold a PhD in Biomedical Engineering from **Universidad Politécnica de Madrid**. My research centers on **deep learning**, **physiological signal processing** (radar, ECG, inertial and wearable sensors), and **clinical decision support systems**. I work on regulatory-compliant AI — Software as a Medical Device — and on continuous learning pipelines for automated model retraining.

Before Ostrava, I was a Research Scholar at **UC Santa Cruz** (Computer Vision Lab) and the **Karlsruhe Institute of Technology** (Computer Vision and Accessibility Lab), **Universidad Politecnica de Madrid** (Center for Biomedical Technology), where I worked on assistive technologies for people with visual impairment and on biomechanical analysis. I have mentored more than 30 undergraduate and master's students across assistive technology, biomedical engineering, and computer science. And now I am co-supervising my first PhD scholar. 

### Current research interests

- **Non-contact vital sign monitoring** — advanced signal processing to estimate vital signs in freely moving people using millimeter-wave radar.
- **Wearables and the data gap in women's health** — most motion, sleep, and physiological datasets carry no hormonal annotation at all. Women's bodies cycle; the data pretends they do not. I am building work that closes this gap.
- **Motion, brain, and hormones** — connecting biochemically confirmed hormonal phase with continuous motion signals, sleep architecture, neuroimaging, and cognitive performance. The premise is simple and largely untested: how we move, sleep, and think is modulated by physiology that current datasets ignore.
- **Assistive technology** — deep learning methods to extract motion characteristics from human activity for rehabilitation and ankle-foot orthotics.

**I am actively looking to connect with researchers interested in the relationship between motion, the brain, and hormones.** If that is your field — or you suspect it should be — please [get in touch](mailto:rey0014@vsb.cz).

I played volleyball for twenty-five years. Retired from the court, not from the discipline: I still move, still need the day to have some physical weight to it. I travel whenever the calendar allows, and I have a well documented preference for anything dark: chocolate, whisky, coffee. The stronger the better.

I value hard work, and I have a genuine tolerance for chaos — some of my best ideas have arrived in the middle of it. Mostly I just like putting my brain to work on problems that resist easy answers.
